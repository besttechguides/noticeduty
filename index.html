Notice queue

The reminder and cooling-off notices the Act will require once in force (expected from January 2027), dated from your settings. Send them in writing, ideally by email or letter, which are normally a “durable medium”. Planned regulations are expected to require a durable medium for reminders.

Sent log

Proof of sending. Keep this. If a customer claims they were never reminded, this is the record you show.

Business settings

Used on every notice. The reminder period must match the period you told customers in your key pre-contract information (s.259(3) and Sch 23 para 10). It counts back from the last cancellation date, not from the payment date.

For plans where the next payment is more than 12 months away, the Act requires an additional, earlier reminder at a “reasonable” time (s.259(6)–(8)). The Act sets no number of days. 30 is our default, not a legal rule.

What the rules require (expected from January 2027)

Digital Markets, Competition and Consumers Act 2024, Part 4 Chapter 2. Not yet in force. Regulations and guidance still to be published will fill in some detail and may change timings.

Reminder notices

A reminder before the renewal payment that closes each six-month period (the first period starts the day after sign-up). With a free or discounted trial, the first reminder is before the first full payment (s.258). If the next payment is more than 12 months away (annual plans), you must also send an additional earlier reminder (s.259(6)–(8)). You send the reminder within the period you stated in your key pre-contract information, counted back from the last cancellation date (s.259(3)–(5)). The required information must be more prominent than anything else in the message, and the contents are set by Schedule 23 Part 3. Planned regulations are expected to add durable-medium and “purpose immediately apparent” rules.

Cooling-off notices

There is a 14-day initial cooling-off period from sign-up (s.264–265). There is a new 14-day renewal cooling-off period when a free or discounted trial turns into a paid one, and when a renewal payment means the next payment is more than 12 months away (s.265(3)). For each renewal cooling-off period, send a cooling-off notice on its first day, or as soon as reasonably practicable after that, and separately from any other information (s.266). Refunds on cooling-off cancellation are left to regulations (s.267).

Cancellation / end-of-contract

Exit must be straightforward. If customers signed up online, they must be able to cancel online (s.260). When they cancel, send an end-of-contract notice in writing on a durable medium that confirms the end date. Send it within 24 hours if they cancelled online, or within 3 working days otherwise, unless regulations set a different period (s.261(4)–(5)). Refund any overpayment. Regulations are expected to set the refund deadline (14 days was proposed).

If you miss one

Sending the required reminders and having proper exit arrangements are implied terms of the contract (s.262). If you breach them, the customer can cancel (s.263). The refunds that follow are set by regulations that are not yet made. You have to prove that you sent a notice (s.272). The CMA can also fine businesses directly for consumer law breaches, up to 10% of global turnover (DMCC Act 2024 Part 3, in force since 6 April 2025). The 2023 impact assessment estimated about 236,000 UK businesses offer in-scope subscriptions.

What is not final yet

Most of Chapter 2 is shown on legislation.gov.uk as prospective (not yet in force). The government announced on 9 August 2026 that the rules will start in January 2027, so they are expected from January 2027, but no day has been fixed in law. No commencement regulations, subscription contract regulations or draft regulations had been published when we checked on 4 October 2026. The regulations are expected to cover the durable medium, refund deadlines, cooling-off refunds and easy-exit detail, and they may change these timings. NoticeDuty applies the wording of the Act and marks the open points. Check GOV.UK and legislation.gov.uk before you rely on a date.
